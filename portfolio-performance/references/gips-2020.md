# GIPS® 2020: the current standards (summary for agents)

**Status (checked October 2026):** the 2020 edition (issued 30 June 2019, effective 1 January 2020) is still current. CFA Institute administers it. It has three chapters: **Firms**, **Asset Owners** and **Verifiers**. Since 2020 there's been an errata (July 2020), a Handbook for Firms (provision-by-provision explanations), a Q&A database, and these guidance statements for firms:
- Benchmarks (effective 1 Apr 2021, revised July 2023)
- OCIO Portfolios (effective 31 Dec 2025)
- Firms Managing Only Broad Distribution Pooled Funds
- Overlay Strategies
- Verifier Independence
- Wrap Fee Portfolios

**Firms must also comply with all guidance statements, interpretations and Q&As**, and must have a procedure for monitoring new ones (1.A.4–1.A.5). Always check gipsstandards.org for anything newer than this summary before advising.

**This is a paraphrased summary.** Provision numbers (e.g. 2.A.23) refer to *GIPS Standards for Firms 2020*. Required wording (compliance statements, the trademark disclosure) must be copied **exactly** from the source document, not from this summary. The text is © CFA Institute.

## What changed since Bacon (2004) — the important differences

| Topic | Bacon / GIPS 1999 | GIPS 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | AIMR and the IPC, country versions (CVGs, TGs) | CFA Institute and the GIPS governing bodies. Country versions converged into one global standard after 2005 |
| Structure | Five sections (input, calculation, composites, disclosure, presentation) | Eight sections: fundamentals; input data and calculation; composite and pooled fund maintenance; composite TWR report; composite MWR report; pooled fund TWR report; pooled fund MWR report; advertising guidelines |
| Pooled funds | Composites only | Pooled funds have their own reports. Pooled funds are split into *broad distribution* (publicly offered) and *limited distribution* (everything else) |
| Money-weighted returns | Only for VC and private equity | Allowed if the firm **controls external cash flows** and the strategy is closed-end, fixed-life, fixed-commitment, **or** illiquid-investment-heavy (1.A.35). Chosen per composite and applied consistently |
| Valuation at flows | Monthly. Revaluation at each flow "anticipated" for 2010 | **Required at all large cash flows** (firm-defined, per composite) from 2010. Monthly valuation at calendar month end. Daily-weighted flows otherwise (2.A.23–24) |
| Value basis | Market value | **Fair value** (from 2011), with a recommended valuation hierarchy (2.B.6) |
| Verification | Expected to become mandatory | **Still optional** but recommended (1.B.3). The 2020 compliance statements differ for verified and unverified firms |
| Notification | None | A **GIPS Compliance Notification Form** filed with CFA Institute when first claiming compliance and every year by 30 June (1.A.38) |
| Risk statistic | Recommended | **3-year annualised ex post standard deviation** (monthly returns) of composite *and* benchmark at each annual period end, required for periods ending on or after 1 Jan 2011 (4.A.1.j) |
| Carve-outs | Cash allocation disclosed. Own cash from 2005 | Allowed **with allocated cash** (separate or synthetic). Must be representative of a standalone portfolio. All carve-outs of that strategy must be included. A separate standalone composite is required if one exists (3.A.15–18; 4.A.6, 4.A.13, 4.C.28) |
| Theoretical (model or backtested) performance | Can't be linked | Still can't be linked to actual performance (1.A.27). It may appear only as clearly labelled **supplemental information**, with methodology and fee disclosures (4.C.48) |
| Advertising | Not covered | Section 8 GIPS Advertising Guidelines |

Bacon's core ethics carry over unchanged: firm-wide compliance, every fee-paying discretionary portfolio in a composite, no cherry-picking, fair representation and full disclosure.

## Section 1: Fundamentals of compliance (key points)

- **Firm-wide only** (1.A.1). The firm is an entity held out to the public as a distinct business (1.A.2). The broadest definition is recommended, including all offices under the same brand (1.B.4).
- An initial claim needs at least **5 years** of compliant history, or since inception if shorter (1.A.3).
- Written policies and procedures, applied consistently, plus monitoring of new guidance and of laws and regulations (1.A.5–6). Where law conflicts with GIPS, follow the law and disclose the conflict.
- **No false or misleading performance information anywhere**, not just in GIPS reports (1.A.7). No "compliant except for…" claims (1.A.8). Don't describe the calculation methodology as "in accordance with GIPS" (1.A.9).
- **Distribution:**
  - Make every reasonable effort to give a GIPS Composite Report to *every* prospective client when they become one, without choosing who gets one (1.A.11). Update it at least every 12 months while they remain a prospect (1.A.12).
  - The same applies to limited-distribution pooled fund prospects, who get either a pooled fund report or a composite report (1.A.13–14). It's optional for broad-distribution funds.
  - Reports must be updated through the latest annual period end within 12 months (1.A.16). Keep evidence of these efforts (1.A.17).
- **Benchmarks** must reflect the mandate, and **price-only benchmarks are prohibited** (1.A.18–19).
- **Material errors:** correct the report and send it to the current verifier, current clients, and prospects who received the erroneous version (1.A.20–21). Disclose for at least a year (4.C.38).
- Maintain lists of composite descriptions (terminated composites stay listed for 5 years), limited-distribution pooled fund descriptions, and broad-distribution funds. Provide them and any GIPS report on request (1.A.22–24).
- **Records:** all supporting data, kept and retrievable (1.A.25). The firm is responsible for third-party data (1.A.26).
- **No linking of actual and theoretical performance** (1.A.27). Organisational changes must not alter history (1.A.28).
- **Non-compliant history:**
  - TWR: may be linked only for periods before the minimum effective compliance date (1.A.29).
  - MWR: non-compliant periods may be shown only if they end before that date (1.A.30).
  - The minimum effective compliance date is **1 Jan 2000**, or **1 Jan 2006** for real estate, private equity and wrap-fee composites and pooled funds.
- **Portability** (1.A.32–33): substantially all decision makers moved, the process remains intact and independent, records support the numbers, and there's no break in the track record. If there *was* a break, show the before and after periods separately and unlinked.
- **Acquisitions:** one year to bring acquired assets into compliance (1.A.34).
- **TWR by default.** MWR only under the 1.A.35 conditions. Choose per composite or fund and apply consistently (1.A.36).
- File the notification form (1.A.38). If verified, assess the verifier's independence (1.A.39).
- **Recommendations:** update reports quarterly, get verified, send current clients the composite report annually, and show the most appropriate track record for a new limited-distribution fund.

## Section 2: Input data and calculation

**Assets:**
- Total firm assets = the fair value of all discretionary and non-discretionary assets managed. This includes non-fee-paying assets and sub-advised assets where the firm chooses the sub-advisor. It excludes advisory-only assets and uncalled committed capital (2.A.1).
- Use only actual assets, measured net of discretionary leverage, with no double counting (2.A.2–4).
- Overlay exposure: use the notional exposure, the underlying value or a target, consistently within each composite (2.A.5–7).

**Accounting and returns:**
- Total returns. Trade-date accounting. Accrual accounting for interest (cash interest may be on a cash basis), with accrued income in both start and end values (2.A.8–10).
- Cash is included even if the firm doesn't control it (2.A.11).
- **Never annualise periods under a year** (2.A.12).
- Returns are after transaction costs (estimates allowed only where actuals are unknown) (2.A.13).
- **Bundled fees:** if trading costs can't be separated, deduct the whole bundled fee (or its trading portion) for gross returns, and the fee portions including management for net (2.A.14).
- Returns are net of discretionary leverage (2.A.15).
- For investments in underlying pooled funds, deduct their fees unless the firm controls them (2.A.17).
- **Risk measures:** the same periodicity and the same methodology for composite and benchmark (2.A.18). Gross-of-fees returns are recommended for risk measures (2.B.7).

**Valuation:**
- Fair value under the firm's composite-specific valuation policy (2.A.19–20).
- If stale or estimated prices are used, assess them and adjust once final values arrive (2.A.21).
- Consistent annual valuation dates, normally calendar year end (2.A.22).
- Recommended hierarchy (2.B.6): (a) unadjusted quoted prices for identical assets in active markets → (b) quoted prices for similar assets → (c) quotes in inactive markets → (d) other observable inputs → (e) unobservable inputs. Disclose material deviations (4.C.30). Present the percentage of assets valued with unobservable inputs if material (4.A.2).

**TWR for portfolios in composites** (not private markets) (2.A.23–24):
- Value at least **monthly**, at calendar month end, and on the date of **all large cash flows**. "Large" is firm-defined per composite as an amount or percentage, single or aggregate.
- Calculate monthly returns, with sub-period returns at large flows if not daily. Use **daily-weighted** external flows otherwise (Modified Dietz), and **geometrically link**.
- Recommended: value at every external flow (2.B.1).

**Pooled funds not in composites** (2.A.25–26): value at least annually, at each subscription and redemption, and at period ends. Net returns are net of total pooled fund fees. Set an inception date for each fund (2.A.27).

**MWR** (2.A.28–29): value at least annually. **Annualised since-inception** returns, using **daily** external cash flows (from 2020). Stock distributions count as external flows.

**Net-of-fees:**
- Actual fees, or a **model fee appropriate to prospective clients** that produces returns **equal to or lower than** those from actual fees (2.A.30–33).
- Performance-fee clawbacks are recognised when repaid (2.A.34).

**Composite returns:**
- At least monthly (2.A.35). Asset-weighted by beginning values, by beginning values plus flows, or by the **aggregate method** (2.A.36).
- Gross returns are after transaction costs. Net returns are after costs and management fees (2.A.37–38).
- Composite MWR aggregates portfolio-level cash flows (2.A.39).

**Other rules:**
- **Private market investments** in composites: quarterly valuation and returns, with daily-weighted flows (2.A.40–42). External valuation is recommended at least every 12 months (2.B.8).
- **Real estate:** external valuation every 12 months (36 if the client agreement allows), or an annual audit with an unmodified opinion, by an independent qualified valuer whose fee isn't contingent on the value (2.A.43–46).
- **Carve-out net returns** use fees representative of the prospect's standalone or multi-asset mandate (2.A.47).
- **Wrap fee:** returns shown to wrap prospects are net of the entire wrap fee (2.A.48).
- **Side pockets:** include discretionary side pockets (2.A.49).
- **Subscription lines of credit:** MWR "without" the line must still include its cash flows (2.A.50).
- Recommended: ex-date dividend accrual, management fee accrual, net of non-reclaimable withholding tax (2.B.3–5).

## Section 3: Composite and pooled fund maintenance

- Create composites for every strategy offered or managed as a segregated account (3.A.1).
- **All actual, fee-paying, discretionary segregated accounts must be in at least one composite.** Non-discretionary portfolios must not be (3.A.2).
- Pooled funds that meet a composite definition must be included (3.A.3). Non-fee-paying portfolios are allowed under the same policies (3.A.4).
- Define composites by **mandate, objective or strategy**. Include everything that fits, regardless of legal structure (3.A.5). Definition changes are never retroactive (3.A.6).
- Include new portfolios on a timely, consistent basis. Use **only full measurement periods** (3.A.7–8).
- Terminated portfolios stay in history through their last full period (3.A.9).
- Moving a portfolio between composites requires a documented client-directed change or a redefinition. History stays with the old composite, and tactical changes don't count (3.A.10).
- Minimum asset levels are enforced and never applied retroactively (3.A.11).
- **Significant cash flow** removal (different from a *large* cash flow):
  - Defined ex ante per composite, **as an amount or percentage only**.
  - Applied consistently (3.A.12).
  - Temporary new accounts can absorb these flows (recommended) and are excluded from composite performance (3.A.13, 3.B.2).
  - *Large* flows trigger revaluation. *Significant* flows are client flows that stop the firm implementing the strategy.
- **Carve-outs:** must include cash and income, either separately or synthetically allocated. They must be representative, include all carve-outs of that strategy, and a standalone composite must exist where standalone portfolios exist (3.A.15–18).
- Composites, funds or carve-outs must not be combined to simulate a strategy (3.A.19).

## Sections 4–7: report contents

**Composite TWR report (4.A.1).** At least 5 years, building to 10, plus:
- annual composite and benchmark returns (partial first and last years from 2011 onwards)
- the number of portfolios (not required if there are five or fewer)
- composite assets and **total firm assets** at each year end
- **internal dispersion** of portfolio annual returns (not required if five or fewer portfolios for the full year), e.g. high/low, range, or asset- or equal-weighted standard deviation
- **3-year annualised ex post standard deviation** of composite and benchmark, from monthly returns (4.A.1.j), with a disclosure if 36 months aren't available (4.C.36)

Also:
- Label the periods, and whether returns are gross or net (4.A.3).
- Show all required information for every benchmark presented (4.A.4).
- If a composite empties, its track record breaks. Show before and after periods separately, unlinked (4.A.5).
- Show the percentage of assets in carve-outs with allocated cash (4.A.6), and the percentage in non-fee-paying portfolios when net returns use actual fees (4.A.7).
- Everything in one currency (4.A.12).
- Supplemental information must relate to the composite, not conflict with the report, and be labelled (4.A.18).
- Overlay and wrap-fee specifics apply (4.A.14–17). Pure gross wrap returns are supplemental.
- **Recommended** (4.B): both gross and net; cumulative, equal-weighted, quarterly or monthly, and annualised returns; annualised SD over periods longer than 3 years; additional ex post risk measures; more than 10 years; proprietary assets as a percentage of composite assets.

**Required disclosures (4.C), in condensed form:**
- the exact compliance statement for verified, verified-with-examination, or unverified firms (4.C.1), plus the trademark disclosure (4.C.2)
- the firm definition, composite description and benchmark description (with periodicity if less than monthly)
- the fee basis (other fees deducted; performance fees or carry; model vs actual fees; the model fee and method)
- separately charged fees, the reporting currency, and the dispersion measure
- the current fee schedule for this prospect type, and the performance-fee or carry description
- composite inception and creation dates, availability of lists and policies
- historical use of leverage, derivatives and shorts (if material), and estimated transaction costs
- significant events, non-compliant periods, firm or composite redefinitions and name changes
- minimum asset levels, and withholding-tax treatment (composite and benchmark)
- law conflicts, carve-out details, and sub-advisor use
- valuation-hierarchy deviations
- no-benchmark reasons, benchmark changes (prospective vs retroactive), custom or portfolio-weighted benchmark details
- the significant-cash-flow policy, and past-firm performance
- material-error corrections
- the five-or-fewer statements, and preliminary estimated values
- changes of return type, additional risk measures (with the risk-free rate named, and whether gross or net)
- overlay and real estate specifics
- **theoretical performance** labelled supplemental, with methodology and fee basis disclosed

**Composite MWR report (Section 5):**
- The **annualised since-inception MWR** through the latest year end (non-annualised if under a year), and the benchmark's since-inception MWR for the same periods (often a **PME**).
- Portfolio count, composite assets and total firm assets.
- With a **subscription line of credit**, show returns both with and without it, unless the principal was repaid within 120 days from called capital and none funded distributions (5.A.2).
- With committed capital: paid-in capital, distributions, committed capital, and the **TVPI, DPI, PIC and RVPI multiples** (5.A.4).
- Disclosures are similar to section 4, plus the benchmark calculation method or PME index, the external cash-flow frequency if not daily, and line-of-credit purpose, size and outstanding amount.

**Pooled fund reports (Sections 6–7)** mirror sections 4–5 for pooled funds, with the pooled fund expense ratio added and no dispersion or portfolio count.

## Section 8: GIPS Advertising Guidelines (key points)

- **Advertisement** = material distributed to more than one party with no contact between firm and reader (websites, fact sheets, brochures and so on). One-on-one presentations and client reporting are excluded.
- Only for firms already compliant. Returns must derive from the corresponding GIPS report. Disclosures must be consistent with it.
- Benchmark total returns of the same type, currency and periods. Other information is allowed only with equal or lesser prominence.
- No annualising under a year. Linking rules mirror section 1.
- **Performance must be shown in one of these formats:**
  - (a) 1-, 3- and 5-year annualised returns (plus since inception if under 5 years)
  - (b) period-to-date plus 1/3/5-year annualised
  - (c) period-to-date plus 5 annual returns
  - (d) annualised over the full period of the GIPS report
  - For MWR: the since-inception MWR.
- Required: gross or net labels, the advertising compliance statement and trademark disclosure (exact wording from the source), and how to obtain a GIPS report.
- **Broad distribution pooled funds:** follow regulatory periods if mandated. Net returns use the specific share class, or the maximum-fee generally available class (or all classes). Disclose the expense ratio and what it includes, the fund description, an appropriate risk measure or narrative, and the benchmark description.

## Guidance Statement on Benchmarks (effective 1 Apr 2021, revised July 2023)

- The benchmark must be an **appropriate total-return** benchmark. Price-only benchmarks may appear **only as labelled supplemental information alongside** a total-return benchmark, and never as the only benchmark. PMEs, commodity indexes and target returns (e.g. an 8% hurdle) count as total return.
- Present benchmark returns for every period in which composite or fund returns are shown (including partial or quarterly periods, and both with and without a line of credit). Same currency.
- Describe each benchmark. If unsure whether it's widely recognised, describe it anyway.
  - **ETF benchmarks:** disclose gross or net, the expense ratio, price vs NAV basis, the close timing, and withholding tax.
- **Custom, blended or multi-asset benchmarks:** disclose components, weights, rebalancing and method, and label the benchmark as custom.
  - Actual-weight (rather than strategic-weight) benchmarks must be disclosed as such.
  - Every component needs a benchmark. Disclose if an asset-class return or target return stands in for one.
  - **Don't blend TWRs with MWRs.** A private-market component of a TWR blend needs a TWR benchmark.
- **Net benchmarks:** never shown when only gross composite returns are presented. Label as a "custom net benchmark" and disclose the method.
- **Peer groups:** allowed if they reflect the mandate. The median fund changes each period, so peer-group standard deviations may not be meaningful. Disclose limitations (4.D.8).
- **Absolute-return strategies:** a risk-free or target benchmark is allowed (the 3-year SD shows the risk mismatch), or no benchmark with an explanation.
- **MWR composites:** use the **PME** method for public indexes (disclose the index, and describe it if not well known). The PME can fail when outperformance and distributions push the hypothetical investment short, so choose a method and apply it consistently. A non-PME benchmark (e.g. a vintage-year peer IRR) may be shown as supplemental information.
- **Benchmark changes:**
  - Prospective: disclose the date and description for as long as prior-benchmark returns are shown, and label the spliced series as custom.
  - **Retroactive:** disclose it, state that the change was retroactive, and keep the disclosure for at least a year.
  - This contrasts with Bacon's "never change retrospectively". GIPS permits it but requires prominent disclosure.
- Withholding-tax materiality is set by policy. Disclose whether the benchmark is net of withholding tax if known.

## Guidance Statement for OCIO Portfolios (effective 31 Dec 2025)

Applies when a firm gives an asset owner **both** strategic investment advice (strategic asset allocation and investment policy) **and** investment management (construction, manager and fund selection, tactical allocation). Advice-only assets count as advisory-only, outside total firm assets. UK fiduciary managers follow the separate *GIPS Standards for Fiduciary Management Providers* for UK pension-scheme tenders.

Every discretionary fee-paying OCIO portfolio must be placed in a **Required OCIO Composite** according to its **strategic** (not tactical) allocation. Composite names should include "OCIO". Use the midpoint when the strategic allocation is a range. Glide-path portfolios change composites as their allocation shifts, and history stays with the old composite.

| Liability-focused (by liability-hedging %) | Hedging / Growth | Total return (by growth %) | Growth / Risk-mitigating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggressive | 0–14 / 86–100 | Aggressive | 86–100 / 0–14 |
| Moderate Aggressive | 15–29 / 71–85 | Moderate Aggressive | 71–85 / 15–29 |
| Moderate | 30–49 / 51–70 | Moderate | 51–70 / 30–49 |
| Moderate Conservative | 50–74 / 26–50 | Moderate Conservative | 26–50 / 50–74 |
| Conservative | 75–100 / 0–25 | Conservative | 0–25 / 75–100 |

- **Recommended classification:** investment-grade fixed income and cash are liability-hedging or risk-mitigating. Hedge funds can go either way (disclose which). Everything else is growth. Policies must be consistent, an asset can't be split between categories, and deviations must be disclosed.
- **Returns:** TWR, with **both gross and net** required. Both are net of transaction costs and of underlying fund and external manager fees. Net is also after the OCIO's own fee.
- Report: actual percentages of hedging/growth or growth/risk-mitigating assets, and of **private market investments and hedge funds**, at each year end (for periods ending on or after 31 Dec 2025). Explain legacy-asset policy and exclusions. Benchmarks may be portfolio-weighted custom blends (with aggregated component disclosure allowed) or a mandate-consistent peer group.
- 5 years building to 10. Provide the relevant Required OCIO Composite report to every OCIO prospect, or tell them none fits. Optional client-type composites (e.g. endowments only) are an allowed exception.

## How to use this alongside Bacon
- **Methodology** (return formulas, risk measures, attribution): Bacon's chapters remain valid and are more detailed than GIPS. GIPS sets *minimums* (monthly, large-flow revaluation, daily-weighted flows) and *disclosure*, not attribution methods.
- **Compliance questions:** use this file (then verify against the current source). Treat `standards.md` as historical rationale.
- **Attribution in GIPS reports:** attribution is optional supplemental information. Apply the EIPC disclosure guidance in `standards.md` and the 4.A.18 supplemental-information rules.
