# Question bank

Each entry gives the question, how to derive the **recommended answer**, the **book rationale**, and **what changes** if the user answers differently. Only ask what the material and conversation haven't already settled. Adapt the wording, and always fill in a concrete recommendation drawn from the user's own material. A generic recommendation is a last resort.

## §1 Audience and setting

**1.1 Who is the primary audience, and what should they do after the deck?**
- *Recommend:* the most senior decision-maker implied by the material, and the single decision or action it points toward, such as "approve", "choose", "adopt", or "fund".
- *Why:* the Question lives in the audience's mind. Without a named audience you can't tell what belongs in S and C (what they know) versus the body (what they don't).
- *If different:* a mixed audience means you aim S and C at the least-informed decision-maker and move the specialist detail to the appendix.

**1.2 What does the audience already know and accept about this subject?**
- *Recommend:* list 2–3 facts from the material you believe they accept, and ask the user to confirm or strike them.
- *Why:* the introduction may contain only accepted truths. Getting this wrong distorts the Question.

**1.3 Live presentation, read-ahead, or both?**
- *Recommend:* live with speaker notes, unless the user mentions emailing, a pre-read, or a board pack.
- *Why:* the book's text-slide rules assume a speaker carries the reasoning. Read-aheads need fuller slide text but keep one idea per slide.
- *If both:* build the live deck, and put outline-form notes on each slide or produce a separate read-ahead version.

**1.4 How much time, or how many slides?**
- *Recommend:* about one slide per 2 minutes, then trim to the Key Line plus one level and move the rest to the appendix.
- *Why:* the pyramid's depth, not the amount of material, should set the length.

**1.5 Output format and any template or brand?**
- *Recommend:* .pptx 16:9. If the user has a template, ask for it.
- *Why:* this is mechanical, so ask only once and never let it block the storyline work.

## §2 Question and Answer (always resolve these)

**2.1 Which of the standard questions does this deck answer?**
- *Recommend:* the pattern that matches the material (see introductions.md):
  - a costed fix points to "Should we approve?"
  - known options point to "Which one?"
  - a mandated change points to "How do we do it?"
  - a failed initiative points to "Why didn't it work, and what now?"
  - phase results point to "What did you find?"
- *Why:* once the Question is fixed, the rest of the structure follows much more easily.
- *If different:* the Key Line type changes. "How" gives steps, "Should I" gives reasons, "Which" gives criteria.

**2.2 What is your answer, in one sentence?**
- *Recommend:* draft it from the material as a specific, disputable claim with a verb and, if possible, a number. For example, "Consolidate the three warehouses into Jurong by Q2 to cut logistics cost 18%."
- *Why:* the top box must answer the Question directly. Vague answers ("We need to optimize logistics") are intellectually blank.
- *If the user doesn't know yet:* switch to bottom-up. Group their material, draw conclusions, and propose the answer that emerges.

**2.3 Is this the answer you can actually defend with what you have?**
- *Recommend:* flag any part of the Answer the material doesn't support, and propose a narrower version that it does support.
- *Why:* an inductive summary must not go beyond what the grouping supports.

## §3 Situation and Complication

**3.1 What's the uncontroversial starting point?**
- *Recommend:* the earliest fact in the material that the audience accepts and that is anchored in time and place.
- *Why:* thinking from the Situation makes the right Complication easier to find.

**3.2 What changed, or went wrong, that makes this worth discussing now?**
- *Recommend:* the tension point in the material: a missed target, a new constraint, a failed attempt, a proposal on the table, or an opportunity.
- *Why:* the Complication must trigger exactly the Question from 2.1. If it doesn't, one of them is wrong.

**3.3 Does anything in your S or C need proof?**
- *Recommend:* move any contested point into the body.
- *Why:* the introduction reminds the audience. It doesn't argue.

**3.4 For analytical decks: what is R1 now, and what should R2 be?**
- *Recommend:* R1 is the measured symptom from the data. R2 is a target the audience would sign off on.
- *Why:* a precise R1→R2 gap defines the Question and rules out false "alternatives".

## §4 Key Line and grouping

**4.1 What question does the Answer raise: why, how, or which?**
- *Recommend:* why → reasons, how → steps, which → criteria, all inferred from the pattern.

**4.2 Here are the 2–5 Key Line points I propose. Are they right?**
- *Recommend:* a concrete, MECE set, each written as a full sentence, with the order named (time, structural, or importance).
- *Why:* keep groupings small (3–4 ideal), all of one kind, and in logical order.
- *Also ask:* "Is anything missing that the audience would expect?" (the exhaustiveness test) and "Do any two of these overlap?" (the exclusivity test).

**4.3 Induction or deduction at the Key Line?**
- *Recommend:* inductive, almost always.
- *Why:* it is easier to absorb. Use deduction only if the audience must follow a chain of logic to accept the conclusion, and then keep it to four steps or fewer.

**4.4 This group of actions: what is their combined effect?**
- *Recommend:* a specific end-state that implies a measurable result.
- *Why:* action groups are summarized by the direct effect of doing them.

**4.5 This group of findings: what do they have in common, and so what?**
- *Recommend:* the shared subject, action, or implication, stated as an inference.
- *Why:* situation groups are summarized by the implication of their similarity.

## §5 Evidence and exhibits

**5.1 For each support point: what evidence proves it, and do you have the data?**
- *Recommend:* the chart type from the five-question table in slides.md, and the specific data series needed. Anything missing becomes `[DATA NEEDED]`.
- *Why:* one message per exhibit, with the message as the title.

**5.2 What should go to the appendix?**
- *Recommend:* everything below the Key Line plus one level, and any detail only specialists will ask about.

## §6 Close

**6.1 What exactly do you want from the room at the end?**
- *Recommend:* a single ask with an owner and a date, for example "Approve $1.2M today so pilot starts 1 March."
- *Why:* the close restates the governing thought and states the next steps concretely.

**6.2 What's the toughest objection you expect?**
- *Recommend:* take the weakest Key Line point and make sure its evidence slide pre-empts that objection.
- *Why:* stress-testing the grouping for completeness often reveals a missing point.
