# Question bank: alignment MCQs and readiness gates

How to use: for the active use case, ask only the questions the conversation and material haven't settled, in the order given (dependency order), one question per message. Replace the generic options with ones derived from the user's material. The first option is the default recommendation; adapt it when the context points elsewhere, and always explain why. Every option should lead to a visibly different output.

Each use case ends with a **readiness gate**: don't produce the main deliverable until every item is settled (by answer or by recorded assumption).

## §0 Shared questions

**0.1 Where are your learners starting from?**
- a) (Rec. when unclear) Novices — no usable mental model yet. *Why:* the most common case for volunteer and intro teaching; needs model-building, slow pace, tutorials. *If different:* competent learners need gap-filling and a faster, manual-like style.
- b) Competent practitioners filling specific gaps.
- c) Mixed — some novices, some experienced (requires extra exercises for advanced learners and pairing).
- d) False beginners — studied it before, rusty (fast pace after a quick refresher).

**0.2 What should learners be able to *do* afterwards?** (options drafted from the material as concrete, verifiable tasks at different Bloom levels — e.g. apply / understand / set up / evaluate). *Why:* objectives with measurable verbs let you write the assessments first.

**0.3 What's the format and time budget?**
- a) (Rec. for first-timers) A single live session of 2–3 hours with exercises. *Why:* short enough to design well; shows whether the approach works.
- b) Full-day or two-day workshop.
- c) Multi-week course.
- d) Self-paced/online.

**0.4 Who delivers it?**
- a) (Rec.) You, live, possibly with a helper.
- b) Several instructors co-teaching.
- c) Other people later (needs detailed instructor notes and maintainability).
- d) Nobody — learners use it alone.

**0.5 How will you know it worked?**
- a) (Rec.) Learners complete a final authentic exercise, plus formative checks along the way.
- b) A quiz or exam at the end.
- c) Learners use the skill at work/school afterwards (follow-up).
- d) Satisfaction only (warn: satisfaction isn't learning).

## §1 Design a lesson, course, or workshop
Ask §0.1–0.5, then:

**1.1 Why are the learners here?**
- a) (Rec. from context) To solve a specific problem in their own work (authentic tasks, early wins).
- b) Required by school or employer (motivation needs more attention).
- c) Curiosity or career change (broader framing, community and next steps).
- d) Mixed.

**1.2 Which persona best matches the typical learner?** (Offer 2–3 drafted personas plus "none of these".) *Why:* "you are not your learners" — a named persona makes later decisions checkable.

**1.3 What setup will learners use?**
- a) (Rec. for adults) Their own laptops, set up to keep working afterwards (needs per-OS instructions and a setup check).
- b) Browser-based environment (no installs; depends on WiFi; nothing left on their machine).
- c) Lab machines prepared in advance.
- d) Unplugged / paper for part of it.

**1.4 What's out of scope?** (multi-select from brainstormed topics). *Why:* intrinsic load can only be reduced by teaching less.

**1.5 Structure?**
- a) (Rec.) One extended example that grows through the lesson, with small exercises (shows integration; pacing via exercises).
- b) Independent modules (easy to reorder and catch up; less integration).
- c) Project-based (high motivation; needs more support).

**1.6 Teaching style for new content?**
- a) (Rec.) Live coding with prediction questions and type-along.
- b) Slides with worked examples.
- c) Flipped: pre-work, then class time on exercises.
- d) Mostly self-paced exercises with help available.

**Mid-work checkpoints:** after personas; after the concept map ("is this the right size?"); after the first formative assessment ("is this the right difficulty?"); after the outline.

**Readiness gate:** persona(s) · starting level · measurable objectives · format and duration · setup approach · out-of-scope list · success measure.

## §2 Review existing material

**2.1 What's the problem you're seeing?**
- a) (Rec. if unknown) No specific problem — general improvement review.
- b) Learners get lost or drop out partway.
- c) Learners finish but can't apply it.
- d) Instructors find it hard to deliver or maintain.

**2.2 Who is it for now, and is that who you want?** (options: current audience as-is / shift to more novice / shift to more advanced / broaden).

**2.3 How much can change?**
- a) (Rec.) Targeted fixes to the highest-impact spots (limit innovation).
- b) Restructure sections but keep content.
- c) Full redesign.
- d) Only wording and exercises.

**2.4 What format do you want the review in?**
- a) (Rec.) Prioritized findings with before/after rewrites.
- b) Annotated copy of the material.
- c) A redesigned outline.

**Readiness gate:** audience · problem · allowed scope of change · output format.

## §3 Exercises and assessments

**3.1 Purpose?**
- a) (Rec.) Formative — quick in-class checks to steer teaching.
- b) Practice/homework.
- c) Summative — graded exam or certification.
- d) Diagnostic pre-assessment.

**3.2 Which misconceptions or objectives must they target?** (options drafted from the material; multi-select).

**3.3 Format?** (multi-select)
- a) (Rec. for in-class) MCQs with diagnostic distractors + Parsons problems.
- b) Faded examples and fill-in-the-blanks.
- c) Tracing / predict-the-output / minimal fix.
- d) Code-and-run or small projects.

**3.4 How will it be graded?**
- a) (Rec. for in-class) Not graded — show of hands / cards / poll.
- b) Auto-graded tests.
- c) Rubric by instructor or peers.

**3.5 How many and how hard?** (e.g. one per 10–15 minutes of teaching, at the level of the matching objective).

**Readiness gate:** purpose · targets · formats · grading · quantity.

## §4 Tutor one learner

**4.1 What do you want out of this?**
- a) (Rec. from context) Be able to do a specific task (say which).
- b) Understand a concept deeply.
- c) Prepare for a test or interview.
- d) Explore — see what the field is about.

**4.2 How do you like to learn?** (not "learning styles" — practical preferences)
- a) (Rec.) Short explanations, then lots of quick questions.
- b) Work through a real problem, with help when stuck.
- c) Longer explanations first, then practice.

**4.3 How much time per session, and how many sessions?**
- a) (Rec.) 20–30 minutes, several sessions spaced over days (spacing beats cramming).
- b) One long session now.
- c) Ongoing, no fixed plan.

**4.4 Pre-assessment:** 2–3 concrete-task questions with three-level answers ("wouldn't know where to start / could struggle through / could do it quickly").

**During tutoring:** every question to the learner is a formative MCQ (or predict-the-output) with diagnostic distractors; one at a time; wait for the answer.

**Readiness gate:** goal · starting level (from pre-assessment) · session length and cadence.

**5.1 Who's the learner?**
- a) Child 6–12. b) Teenager. c) Adult novice. d) Junior colleague.
(Recommend block-based tools for a and b.)

**5.2 What does the learner want to make or do?** (options drafted from context; recommend something tangible and personally meaningful).

**5.3 How much time do you have together?**
- a) (Rec.) 30–45 minutes, once or twice a week. b) Weekend blocks. c) Ad hoc.

**5.4 What's been tried, and what went wrong?** (options: lost interest / got stuck on setup / too hard / nothing yet).

**Readiness gate:** learner profile · goal project · time budget · known obstacles.

## §6 Prepare to deliver

**6.1 What do you need most?**
- a) (Rec.) A live-coding plan with prediction points and likely mistakes.
- b) Speaker notes and timing.
- c) Feedback on a recording or rehearsal.
- d) Confidence and nerves — how to practise.

**6.2 Is this your first time teaching this material?**
- a) (Rec.) Yes → stick to the plan, no improvising. b) No → plan for adapting to questions.

**6.3 Setup?** (projector and room / online screen share / both)

**Readiness gate:** deliverable type · experience with the material · delivery setup.

## §7 Workshop/event logistics

**7.1 Format?** a) (Rec.) In person, one site. b) Online, one group. c) Several sites connected, each with local helpers. d) Hybrid room + remote (warn: remote participants get neglected).

**7.2 Size and helpers?** a) (Rec.) ≤ 30 learners, 1 helper per ~8–10. b) Larger, few helpers. c) Small group, no helpers.

**7.3 Who's attending?** open registration / one organization / invited cohort / group sign-ups.

**7.4 What do you need produced?** (multi-select) schedule · setup instructions + reminder email · pre-workshop questionnaire · helper briefing · code of conduct · checklists · post-event survey.

**Readiness gate:** format · size and helpers · audience · deliverables · date/time constraints.

## §8 Online/video/self-paced

**8.1 Mode?** a) (Rec.) Cohort-based with deadlines and small live groups. b) Fully self-paced. c) Flipped (pre-work + live sessions). d) Recorded talk or screencast series.

**8.2 Scale?** a) Small (≤ 30). b) Medium (hundreds, needs auto-grading). c) Massive.

**8.3 What platform do learners already use?** (options from context; recommend what needs the least new learning).

**8.4 Assessment?** auto-graded / peer review / instructor feedback / none.

**Readiness gate:** mode · scale · platform · assessment approach · episode length.

## §9 Onboarding and mentoring engineers

**9.1 Who's joining?** a) (Rec. from context) Experienced engineers new to this codebase. b) Junior engineers. c) Non-engineers (analysts, PMs) who need to code a little. d) Mixed.

**9.2 What must they do by end of week 1 / month 1?** (options drafted: ship a small change end to end / own an on-call rotation / understand system architecture / deliver a feature).

**9.3 What exists already?** docs / buddy system / nothing / outdated wiki.

**9.4 Delivery?** a) (Rec.) Self-guided checklist + mentor + pairing. b) Cohort classes. c) Mostly docs.

**Readiness gate:** role persona · week-1 and month-1 objectives · existing assets · delivery model.

## §10 Tutorials and docs

**10.1 Is this a tutorial or a reference?**
- a) (Rec. for "getting started") Tutorial — novices building a mental model.
- b) Reference/manual — competent users looking things up.
- c) How-to — one task, for someone who knows the basics (minimal manual page).

**10.2 What task will the reader complete?** (options drafted from the product).

**10.3 Reader's environment?** (OS, tools, prior knowledge options).

## §11 Motivation/accessibility/inclusivity audit

**11.1 What should be audited?** (multi-select) materials · delivery/classroom behaviour · venue/platform · recruitment and marketing · code of conduct.

**11.2 Known needs of your learners?** none known (use baseline) / specific needs (say which) / ask them via pre-survey (Rec.).

**11.3 How much change now?** a) (Rec.) The top 2–3 fixes. b) A full plan to phase in over several events.

**Readiness gate:** audit scope · known needs · change budget.

## §12 Evaluation and feedback

**12.1 What do you want to know?** a) (Rec.) Whether learners are following during the lesson (formative). b) Whether they learned it (summative). c) Whether they use it later. d) How the instructor can improve.

**12.2 How much effort can learners give?** a) (Rec.) Seconds (minute cards, one-up/one-down). b) A 5-minute survey. c) A follow-up months later.

**Readiness gate:** question to answer · feedback budget · who acts on the results.
