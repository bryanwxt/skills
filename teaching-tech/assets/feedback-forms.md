# Feedback forms

## Minute cards (before each break)
Two colours of sticky note or a two-field form:
- **Green:** one thing that was good or useful.
- **Red:** one thing that was too fast, too slow, confusing, irrelevant, or still unanswered.
Cluster during the break; address the top 1–2 issues after it.

## One up, one down (end of day)
Go around the room alternating: one positive, one thing to change. No repeats. Once the easy comments are used up, the real ones come out. Non-anonymous — pair it with minute cards.

## 2×2 grid (feedback on a teaching segment)
| | Content | Presentation |
|---|---|---|
| Went well | | |
| Could improve | | |

## Specific questions for getting feedback on your teaching
- What is one thing I could have done to make this more effective?
- If you could go over one thing again, what would it be?
- What was the most confusing moment?
- What will you try first when you get home?

## Post-event survey (keep it short)
1. What can you now do that you couldn't before? (free text)
2. How confident are you doing <objective 1> on your own? (not at all / with help / on my own)
3. Which part was most useful? Least useful?
4. What should we change next time?
5. Would you be interested in helping at a future event? (yes/no + contact)

## Evaluation levels (Kirkpatrick)
1. **Reaction** — how learners felt (satisfaction; doesn't equal learning).
2. **Learning** — what they can now do (formative/summative results).
3. **Behaviour** — whether they use it afterwards (follow-up survey after 2–3 months; low response and bias).
4. **Results** — effect on their work or organization.
Be clear about which level you're measuring and its limits.
