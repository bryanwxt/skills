# Phase 6: Rehearse and Deliver

The speaker is the show; slides only support them. Produce a **rehearsal kit** containing the items below that apply, then offer it to the user. You can't rehearse for them, but you can make rehearsing cheap.

## Rehearsal kit (all types)

1. **The spoken introduction**, written out word for word. Rehearse it until it's natural, and check the Question still holds for this audience.
2. **A timing plan** from the storyboard, with checkpoints ("by 7:00 you should be on slide 9") and the 2–3 slides to cut if you're running late.
3. **Q&A prep:** the likely hard questions, each with a 2-sentence answer and a pointer to its backup slide. For Decide decks, include "what if we say no?"
4. **Dry-run protocol:**
   - Run it at least once, out loud, timed.
   - If possible, record it and watch it back.
   - Collect feedback on a 2×2 grid: what went well / what to improve × content / delivery.
   - Write your own self-assessment first.
   - Ask your listeners *specific* questions, e.g. "What was the main point? What would you decide?"
   - If a listener can't state the governing thought afterwards, fix the storyline, not the delivery.
5. **Pre-wiring (Decide):** who to show the storyline to before the meeting, and what you need from each person.

## Delivery rules

- **Open (10–30 s):** who you are, the question, and what the audience will leave with. **Close (10–30 s):** the answer, the ask or next step, and nothing new.
- **Don't read the slides.** Don't contradict them either. Say what the slide doesn't show.
- **Repeat audience questions** before answering. **Don't bluff.** "I don't know, and I'll find out by Friday" builds credibility.
- **Nervous habits:** replace them with something else rather than trying to suppress them.

## Teach: add

- **Live coding:**
  - Type the code; never paste it.
  - Say what you type, then point at the result and say it again.
  - Ask for a prediction before each run.
  - Make mistakes and diagnose them out loud.
  - Mirror the learners' setup: default prompt and theme, large font (about 60–70 columns by 20–30 rows), notifications off.
  - Announce every switch between tools.
- **Room practices:**
  - Sticky notes: green for "done", red for "help".
  - Minute cards before each break (one good thing, one confusing thing). Act on them straight after the break.
  - One up / one down at the end.
  - Pair learners, and never touch a learner's keyboard.
- **Co-teaching:**
  - Agree who teaches which section, with stretches of at least 10–15 minutes each.
  - Agree hand signals.
  - The partner who isn't leading watches the room and doesn't interrupt.
  - Debrief afterwards.
- **Logistics checklist:**
  - Setup instructions sent 3+ days ahead, plus a reminder.
  - A check command to run at the door.
  - A shared notes doc.
  - A code of conduct, announced at the start.
  - Accessibility needs asked about in advance.
  - Spare adapters, and an offline fallback (installers or a hosted environment).
  - Helpers assigned (about 1 per 8–10 learners for hands-on sessions).
- **Technical dry run:** a helper or colleague does every exercise from a fresh account on each operating system the learners use, following only the slides and handouts.
- **For a one-off audience:** try at most one classroom technique that's new to them.

## After

- Record what to **do again** and what to **do differently**. Update the brief/storyline artifacts so the next version starts from them.
- Teach: judge the session by the capstone results and whether learners change their behaviour, not only by satisfaction ratings.
