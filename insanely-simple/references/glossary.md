# Glossary and quote bank

## Vocabulary

- **Simplicity / Complexity:** capitalised in the book as opposing forces. Simplicity is the natural human preference. Complexity is the seductive default that never dies and finds a home in hierarchies, processes and people.
- **The Simple Stick:** the act of rejecting work, or a project's premise, because it doesn't distill the idea to its essence. It can inspire or club. "Being hit with the Simple Stick" means having your work rejected for being too complicated.
- **Champion of Simplicity:** the person who stands up for it, guided by head and heart, willing to step on toes. Simplicity doesn't happen without one.
- **Brains + Common Sense:** the two parents of simplicity. Smart organisations still lose them to Complexity.
- **All-or-nothing:** simplicity isn't a buffet. Partial adoption loses.
- **Small groups of smart people:** the core organisational unit. Both words matter.
- **Mercy invitation:** inviting someone to a meeting to be nice when they aren't needed.
- **The two laws of small:** (1) output quality is inversely proportional to the number of people involved. (2) Output quality rises with the involvement of the ultimate decision maker.
- **Ultimate decision maker:** whoever really holds approval authority, not necessarily the CEO. They must be in from the briefing and at checkpoints.
- **Hard reset:** Jobs judged each meeting fresh. Yesterday's blow-up didn't carry over.
- **Rotating turret:** an insider's name for Jobs's attention locking onto the source of a foolish comment before he fired.
- **Being "Steved":** on the receiving end of a Jobs critique. Not a death sentence.
- **Reality distortion field:** Jobs's ability to make people believe in improbable outcomes (and his sometimes inflated praise).
- **Product proliferation:** an overlapping glut of models or tiers, a hallmark of Complexity.
- **Sea of choices:** too much choice, which becomes a liability ("a sea of choices is no choice at all").
- **Ease of purchase:** the author's counterpart to ease of use: how simply a customer can choose and buy.
- **The smart timeline:** a plan with "not quite enough time" (after Bernstein). In the author's field, about 3 months from briefing to live.
- **1.0 → 2.0 recycling:** ship a focused 1.0, then use the cut features to seed 2.0, renewing leadership each cycle.
- **Iconic image:** a single conceptual image that captures an idea's essence. Not decoration.
- **One button:** the official symbol of simplicity. "Three is more than one."
- **Obviousness principle:** prefer the name or solution that is instantly understood, even if it seems too easy.
- **The curse of Simplicity:** finished simple work looks obvious or easy, which hides the effort and invites shallow imitation.
- **Human-speak / real-speak:** communicating in everyday words and benefits rather than specs.
- **Tone:** the combined effect of words and images. It's what makes communication "human", more than images of people.
- **10x:** Jobs's bar, held since NeXT: meaningful change is an order-of-magnitude improvement, or a new category.
- **The intersection of technology and liberal arts:** Jobs's framing of Apple's identity and his basis for judgment.
- **Healthy skepticism:** weighing expert opinion in context, and treating it as advice, not orders.
- **Overwhelming force:** championing an idea with every available resource, because a tie goes to Complexity.
- **Proxy enemy:** attacking one representative (Intel, later Microsoft) to attack a whole category.
- **Brand bank:** a brand works like an account. Hits deposit trust and failures withdraw it, and a high balance gets you through crises.
- **Ecosystem of Simplicity:** consistent simplicity across every touchpoint: ad → site → store → unboxing → use → support.
- **Monument to Simplicity:** the author's description of Apple itself as Jobs's greatest creation.

## Quote bank (short, attributed; use sparingly)

Jobs (public statements, as cited in the book):
- "Innovation is saying no to a thousand things." (WWDC 1997)
- "We're the biggest start-up on the planet." (D8, 2010, on Apple having zero committees)
- "I don't see why you have to change as you get big."
- "To me, marketing is about values." (Think different internal launch, 1997)
- "Just show me the ads!"
- "Put the software in the box."
- "People don't know what they want until you show it to them." (BusinessWeek, 1998)
- "Sometimes when you innovate, you make mistakes." (followed by: admit them quickly and move on)
- "Simple can be harder than complex." (the book's epigraph, which says clean thinking takes hard work)

Others:
- Leonard Bernstein: great things need "a plan, and not quite enough time."
- Apple II brochure (1977): "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."
- Henry Ford (as Jobs cited him): people would have asked for "a faster horse."
- Chiat/Day T-shirt: "Good enough is not enough."
- The author's moral for minimising: "When in doubt, minimize."

Keep any quotation in agent output to one short line and attribute it. Paraphrase everything else.
