# Frameworks and quote bank

## Frameworks at a glance

**Nine elements of a persuasive script (Scene 1):** headline · passion statement · three key messages · metaphors and analogies · demonstrations · partners · customer evidence and third-party endorsements · video clips (≤2–3 min) · props, flip charts and show-and-tell.

**Aristotle's five-point outline:** (1) a story or statement that arouses interest → (2) a problem or question → (3) the solution → (4) the specific benefits → (5) the call to action.

**Headline test:** ≤140 characters · subject-verb-object · specific · personal benefit · repeated in every channel.

**Four-question elevator pitch:** What do you do? · What problem do you solve? · How are you different? · Why should I care?

**Road map pattern:** preview 3 (or 4) points → cover point 1 → summarise → … → recap all → repeat the headline.

**Villain → hero pattern:** "Why do we need this?" → the state of the category and its pain → raise and answer objections → reveal the hero → benefits in plain words.

**Mayer's multimedia principles:** multimedia (words + pictures) · contiguity (words next to the matching picture) · split attention (narrate rather than put text on screen) · coherence (cut extraneous material) · signalling (direct the eye with arrows and highlights).

**Picture superiority effect:** about 10% recall after 72 h for oral information alone, about 65% with a picture added (as cited from Medina).

**Numbers test:** specific · relevant · contextual, plus an analogy where useful.

**Word test:** simple · concrete · emotional. Check readability: words per sentence, lexical density, hard words, fog index.

**Kawasaki's demo test:** short · simple · sweet · swift (≤15 s per step) · substantial.

**Showstopper recipe:** one theme → a physical, visual or personal embodiment → build-up → twist → rehearsed → reinforced in every channel.

**Stage presence:** eye contact · open posture · purposeful gestures. **Voice:** inflection · pauses · volume · rate.

**Video review checklist:** eye contact · body language · filler words · vocal delivery · energy (aim for 7–9/10).

**Five steps to tossing the script:** full sentences in notes → highlight key words → keep only key words → one idea per slide → rehearse with no notes.

**Bucket method:** list the questions → group them into about 7 buckets → one strong answer per bucket → listen for trigger words → answer confidently with eye contact.

**Dress rule:** a little better than everyone else, appropriate to the culture.

**Glitch rule:** acknowledge, smile, don't apologise, keep going. "It goes wrong only if you let it."

## Vocabulary

- **Reality distortion field:** Jobs's ability to make people believe in almost anything through passion and delivery.
- **Stevenote:** a Jobs keynote.
- **Slideument:** a slide trying to be a document (Garr Reynolds).
- **"One more thing":** Jobs's closing-surprise signature. Use it sparingly.
- **"Holy shit" moment:** the book's term for the scripted showstopper. In professional writing call it a *showstopper* or *signature moment*.
- **Infotainment:** informing and entertaining together.
- **Messianic purpose:** a drive to "put a dent in the universe" and make lives better.
- **Antagonist / conquering hero:** the problem or enemy, and your solution.
- **Rule of three:** grouping content in threes for memory and rhythm.
- **Anaphora:** repeating a phrase at the start of successive sentences.
- **Kanso:** the Zen principle of simplicity.
- **Chief Demonstration Officer:** Cisco's demo sidekick role.
