# Case library

These are the book's examples, paraphrased and tagged by scene [S#]. Use them as illustrations ("per Gallo's account"), keep quotes short, and don't reproduce long transcripts.

## Apple / Jobs keynotes
- **Macworld 2007: iPhone** [S4, S5, S6, S13, S14] — Jobs lists past revolutions (Mac 1984, iPod 2001), promises "three revolutionary products" (a widescreen iPod, a phone, an internet communicator), and repeats them until the audience realises it's one device. "Apple reinvents the phone" is repeated about five times. Then he sets up the villain (smartphones that are not so smart and hard to use: plastic keyboards, a stylus) and the hero (multi-touch). The delivery builds in volume and pace up to the reveal, with a rotary-dial iPod gag slide as comic relief. The book calls it his best keynote.
- **Macworld 2008: MacBook Air** [S4, S8, S13, S17] — "Something in the air" teaser. "The world's thinnest notebook", used word for word across the keynote, website, press release and CNBC interview. Pulled from a manila envelope. One theme per slide (magnetic latch, display, keyboard, a board smaller than a pencil). Otellini hands over the tiny chip.
- **iPod launch, 2001** [S7, S9, S13, S14] — A price-per-song comparison of portable players, then "1,000 songs in your pocket", "the size of a deck of cards", and the iPod pulled from his pocket. The key line is delivered almost at a whisper.
- **Macintosh launch, 1984** [S6, S7, S13] — A Big Blue history speech casting IBM as the villain, the 1984 ad, then the Mac pulled from a bag and "speaking for itself" ("never trust a computer you can't lift").
- **Intel switch, WWDC 2005** [S2, S5, S12] — Framed as the Mac's third transition. "Why?" answered plainly: we can't build the products we envision on the PowerPC road map. Reveal: the demo machine had been running on Intel all along.
- **Intel iMac, Macworld 2006** [S11] — Paul Otellini walks out in an Intel cleanroom "bunny suit" with a wafer: "Intel is ready."
- **Microsoft deal, Macworld 1997** [S11] — Gates on a giant screen to boos. Jobs told the audience to let go of the idea that Apple wins only if Microsoft loses.
- **Unibody MacBooks, 2008** [S11, S12] — Jony Ive explains the manufacturing. Frames passed around the audience while Jobs stays silent ("we need them back").
- **iPhone 3G, WWDC 2008** [S8, S9, S12] — Eleven slides, ten of them photos. A birthday-cake photo for the first anniversary. A 3G vs EDGE page race (21 s vs 59 s), with Jobs smiling silently through it. "Twice as fast at half the price." "Amazingly zippy."
- **"Let's Rock", 2008** [S8, S18] — First slide: "The reports of my death are greatly exaggerated." Then "Music". The Genius explanation done with cloud and arrow line drawings.
- **iPhone at 200 days** [S9] — 4M units ÷ 200 days = 20,000 a day. Second in share to RIM, and equal to the next three combined.
- **Safari 2003 / Safari for Windows 2007** [S6, S12] — "Why do we need our own browser?" Speed and innovation as the villains. A side-by-side load test (6.64 s vs 13.56 s).
- **GarageBand** [S4, S12] — "A major new pro music tool. But it's for everyone." John Mayer plays while Jobs layers instruments. Later a playful "Supersecret Apple Rumors" podcast demo.
- **Photo Booth, 2005** [S12, S18] — Silly self-portraits with "teenage effects".
- **The Starbucks prank** [S12, S17] — A Google Maps demo ends with a call to Starbucks ordering 4,000 lattes. It was in his notes, scripted.
- **OS 9 funeral, WWDC 2002** [S18] — Casket, smoke, organ music, a eulogy, a rose.
- **"Hell froze over", 2003** [S18] — The slide that introduced iTunes for Windows.
- **The clicker failure, 2007** [S18] — Jobs filled the time with the Woz TV-jammer prank story.
- **The Flickr failure, 2008** [S18] — "Flickr isn't serving up photos on that one", then on with full enthusiasm.
- **Dropping "interim", 2000** [S13] — Saved as "one more thing", preceded by thanks to the team.
- **Thanking teams and families, 2007** [S11] — "Would all the folks who worked on these products please stand up."
- **Noah Wyle as Jobs, 1999** [S11, S18] — The actor opened the keynote, and the real Jobs "coached" him.
- **Stanford, 2005** [Encore] — Three stories (connecting the dots, love and loss, death), humour, anaphora, and "Stay hungry, stay foolish" repeated three times.
- **Recruiting Sculley** [S3] — Sugared water versus a chance to change the world.
- **"Think Different" ad** [S3] — Jobs was moved to tears watching the final cut.
- **The 2×2 product grid** [S1] — Jobs's whiteboard thinking: Apple cut ~40 products down to four.
- **Mac vs PC ads** [S7] — Villain vs hero in 30 seconds. Microsoft's "I'm a PC" response lacked a villain.
- **iPhone app ads (Shazam)** [S7] — Problem and solution in 30 seconds.
- **Apple's mission statement** [S10] — Concrete and emotive, unlike committee jargon.

## Non-Apple
- **Stan Sigman (AT&T), Macworld 2007** [S14] — Hands in pockets, read from note cards in a monotone, lost the room. Bloggers asked "Who's Mr. Note Card?"
- **Phil Schiller, Macworld 2009** [Int. 2] — Successfully applied the full toolkit.
- **Chip-IP CEO** [S2] — A jargon opener replaced with "hold up your phones".
- **Core 2 Duo sales scripts** [S2] — "Two engines" (lost sale) vs "two brains, so you can… " (won sale).
- **SanDisk 12 GB card** [S9] — 50 billion transistors as ants circling the globe twice, and music for a trip to the moon and back.
- **Intel Core i7** [S9] — Ithaca vs Europe shrunk to Ithaca's size.
- **IBM Roadrunner petaflop** [S9] — A 1.5-mile stack of laptops, picked up word for word by the media.
- **$700B bailout** [S9] — Expressed as lattes, iPhones, per-person checks and college tuitions.
- **TravelMuse, DEMO 2008** [S6] — Category → problem (the 95% of trip planning before booking) → solution.
- **LanguageLine elevator pitch** [S6] — The four-question format.
- **Al Gore, *An Inconvenient Truth*** [S6, S9] — The CO₂ graph and the mechanical lift: "off the charts".
- **Google's one-liner to Sequoia** [S4] — Access to the world's information in one click.
- **Southwest triangle / Cranium napkin** [S1] — Ideas simple enough to fit on a napkin.
- **Wal-Mart start-up pitch** [S1] — A day of whiteboarding produced 5 slides for 15 minutes.
- **IPO chip demo** [S1] — The CEO played music from a fingernail-sized chip, and the IPO succeeded.
- **California Strawberry Commission** [S1] — "We brought the farmers to you" video.
- **The organic farmer's hug** [S13] — A personal story beat all the statistics.
- **Cisco: Chambers and Grubb** [S12, S14] — A "chief demonstration officer" with scripted banter. Chambers walks among the audience like a preacher.
- **Marco Montemagno** [S12] — Audience activities, volunteers, table tennis on stage.
- **The CES CEO whose clicker and laptop died** [S15] — Carried on without slides because he'd rehearsed contingencies.
- **The $25,000 slides** [S15] — Polished design, no rehearsal, a public stumble.
- **Caroline Kennedy's fillers** [S15] — "You know" 30+ times in two minutes.
- **Hillary Clinton's confirmation hearing** [S15] — The bucket method in action.
- **Jack Welch** [S10] — "Take me through the basics." Impatient with jargon.
- **Suze Orman** [S10] — Simplicity as respect for the listener.
- **The deli video-billboard pitch** [S10] — A jargon-wrapped pitch for screens in delis.
- **Joel Osteen** [S17] — Four days of prep. Glances at notes but never stands behind the lectern.
- **Jobs's wardrobe** [S16] — Bow tie in 1984, a suit for the bank, a mock turtleneck once he was the brand.
